the 6502 on two breadboards

{% include polaroid-6502.html id="scribble-lightbox" class="scribble__photo" caption="boots up, says hello" %}

A W65C02 running at a crystal's pace, an EEPROM holding the program, 32K of RAM, one 74HC00 doing the glue logic, and a 16×2 LCD that boots up and says hello. Everything is point-to-point jumper wire, which is exactly the point: nothing sits between you and the bus.

It is the best teacher I know for the things cloud consoles hide: address decoding, timing, what a byte actually costs. The "it just works" in my day job started out as a wire like one of these.

the bonsai

The bonsai on the desk is the slowest feedback loop I own. A cut made today shows up as a branch in two years, and there is no rolling back. It is a good antidote to sprint thinking, and a reminder that the systems worth building are the ones you are still tending long after the launch.

how this notebook is drawn

The page is plain HTML and CSS with a small amount of JavaScript that draws the wobbly frames at runtime using Rough.js. The doodles are drawn once at build time and shipped as static paths. Nothing loads from a CDN, and a strict Content-Security-Policy keeps it that way. The handwriting is Excalifont, the body text is Nunito, and it all builds with Jekyll on GitHub Pages.

The moon turns it into a chalkboard. If the drawing-in gets in the way, the wave button turns the motion down.
